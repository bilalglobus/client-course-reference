Course overview

What is the Job Hazard Analysis (JHA) course?

The Job Hazard Analysis (JHA) course from the American Institute of Safety Professionals is an 8-hour, self-paced online course that teaches the most widely used hazard identification method in occupational safety: breaking a job into steps, identifying the hazards in each step and deciding the controls that make it safe. Referenced in OSHA 3071, the method is also known as Job Safety Analysis (JSA) or Activity Hazard Analysis (AHA).

Where risk assessments, HAZOP studies and fault trees look at whole systems, JHA works at the level of the task — it is the last analytical check before work begins. Hands-on exercises use real scenarios from manufacturing, construction, oil and gas, maintenance and general industry.

What you'll learn

How does a Job Hazard Analysis work?

You'll learn the full JHA lifecycle in five steps — not just how to fill in the form — and how to run JHA as a programme across your organisation.

  1. 1 Select the jobPrioritise jobs by injury and illness rates, severity potential, new or modified procedures, near misses and infrequently performed tasks.
  2. 2 Break it into stepsUse observation, worker interviews and procedure review to capture each step in sequence, at the right level of detail.
  3. 3 Identify the hazardsLook at every step for energy sources (mechanical, electrical, chemical, thermal, gravitational, pressure), exposure pathways, environmental conditions and human factors.
  4. 4 Control the hazardsApply the hierarchy of controls — elimination, substitution, engineering, administrative — with PPE as the last resort.
  5. 5 Communicate and reviewBrief the crew before work with toolbox talks and sign-off, then update the JHA after new equipment, procedure changes, incidents or on a set cycle.

What is Job Hazard Analysis (JHA)?

Job Hazard Analysis is a method for making a task safe before it starts: you break the job into steps, identify the hazards in each step and decide the controls for each hazard. OSHA describes the method in its publication OSHA 3071.

Is JHA the same as JSA or AHA?

Yes. Job Hazard Analysis (JHA), Job Safety Analysis (JSA) and Activity Hazard Analysis (AHA) are different names for the same methodology. This course covers all of them.

What does the course cover?

The full JHA methodology, not only form completion: selecting and prioritising jobs, breaking jobs into steps, identifying hazards, applying the hierarchy of controls, documenting the JHA, communicating it to workers, reviewing and updating it, and running JHA as a programme. There are 8 core modules and 4 optional modules.

How long does the course take?

The course has a minimum of 8 instructional hours. It is fully self-paced, and most learners finish within one month. Enrolment is open from January to December 2026.

Who should take this course?

Safety and HSE officers, supervisors and foremen, safety managers, workers who contribute task knowledge, safety committee members, trainers, consultants, and candidates preparing for the CHSO, CSSP or CSS certifications.

Why do supervisors need JHA training?

Pre-task JHA is a frontline responsibility. Supervisors and foremen lead the analysis and the pre-task briefing with their crew, so they need to know the method well.

Do I need any experience to enrol?

No. Basic safety awareness helps but is not required. You don't need prior JHA experience or an academic degree, but you do need working English.

By one online, open-book exam through your assigned student portal. You need 70% or higher to pass, and your result is emailed straight after the exam. If you don't pass, you have one month to review the materials and can retake the exam up to three more times.
